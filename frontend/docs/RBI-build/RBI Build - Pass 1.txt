# Fluid CX — RBI Conduct Intelligence: Master Research Context Pack

**Product spine throughout:** Regulation → Obligation → Customer Interaction Signal → Control Owner → Evidence → Recommended Action.

## 1. One-paragraph story of the research chain

The seven files trace a single argument from the outside in. Perplexity Research (Pass 0) and Claude Pass 1 together map the post-November 2025 RBI customer-conduct rulebook for Indian Scheduled Commercial Banks and NBFCs — roughly 39 instruments anchored on six primary directions, with adjacent DPDP, FREE-AI, SEBI and IRDAI regimes. Pass 2 decomposes that map into 38 named obligations (OBL-001 to OBL-038) across 8 conduct themes, each linked to a business process, a Business Process Owner, an Accountable Executive, and one of five executive lenses (L1–L5). Pass 3 Parts A and B then assess where Fluid CX is honestly the primary control, where it co-controls with the 2nd Line of Defence, where it is merely adjacent, and where it should not claim to help at all — producing a 13 / 17 / 7 / 1 distribution that defines the credible sales boundary. Pass 4A converts the FULL and PARTIAL ratings into a 30-row Integrated Control Register that a CRO and Head of Operational Risk would recognise, with explicit Three-Lines-of-Defence ownership and monitoring outputs. Pass 4B is a two-page CRO investment-case framing addressed from a fictional Head of Conduct Risk to the CRO, arguing the structural case for funding interaction-level monitoring in Q2 2026 ahead of the 30 June 2026 and 1 July 2026 cliff edges.

## 3. The key RBI regulations that matter for the dashboard

**Anchor on these six primary instruments** (cite by reference and effective date):

- **RBI (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025 — RBI/DOR/2025-26/170.** Issued 28 Nov 2025, **in force**. The consolidated rulebook that subsumed the 2015 Master Circular on Customer Service and the 2014 Charter of Customer Rights. The parent regulation for most disclosure, suitability, recovery and grievance obligations.
- **RBI (Internal Ombudsman) Directions, 2026 — RBI/CEPD/2025-26/381 (Banks) and /384 (NBFCs).** Issued 14 Jan 2026, **in force**, with key staffing/CMS clauses 7(2), 14(2), 14(4) hard-dated to **30 June 2026**. Mandates IO independent review of every rejected complaint, automated CMS auto-escalation, quarterly RCA to the Customer Service Committee of the Board.
- **RB-Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 — Press Release 2025-2026/1936.** Notified 16 Jan 2026, **effective 1 July 2026**. Replaces RB-IOS 2021. No monetary ceiling on disputes; consequential-loss cap raised from ₹20 lakh to ₹30 lakh; harassment/mental-anguish cap raised from ₹1 lakh to ₹3 lakh; mandatory Principal Nodal Officer.
- **RBI (Authentication Mechanisms for Digital Payment Transactions) Directions, 2025 — RBI/2025-26/79.** Issued 25 Sept 2025, **effective 1 April 2026** (cross-border CNP from 1 Oct 2026). Issuer accountability for losses from weak authentication; reshapes fraud-dispute scripts, customer-liability communications, SMS/IVR/email alerts.
- **Draft RBI (Commercial Banks / NBFCs / HFCs – RBC) Second Amendment Directions, 2026 — uniform recovery framework.** First draft 12 Feb 2026; **revised draft mid-May 2026**; proposed effective 1 July 2026. **Draft / proposed** — not yet binding. Adds 8 distinct recorded-interaction obligations on Collections including mandatory call recording with prior intimation, time-window restriction (08:00–19:00), prohibition on contacting non-borrowers, mandatory borrower-distress identification, graduated escalation.
- **RBI Statement on Developmental and Regulatory Policies — 6 Feb 2026.** **Supervisory signal, not binding.** Announces the three draft frameworks (anti-mis-selling, recovery conduct, revised limited liability). Useful as the "regulator-said-it-first" anchor.

**Adjacent regimes worth naming (in-force unless flagged):**

- **RBI (Commercial Banks – RBC) Amendment Directions, 2025 — RBI/2025-26/103** (4 Dec 2025; effective 1 April 2026): para 86A on customer choice / no negative consent for ATM, internet, mobile, cheque book.
- **RBI Digital Lending Directions, 2025 — RBI/2025-26/36** (8 May 2025, in force): KFS, cooling-off, recovery-agent intimation, data minimisation.
- **RBI (Commercial Banks / NBFCs – Managing Risks in Outsourcing) Directions, 2025 — RBI/DOR/2025-26/171 and /363** (28 Nov 2025): bank remains responsible for grievance redressal of outsourced services; IT-outsourcing contract compliance deadline 10 April 2026.
- **RBI (Settlement of Claims in respect of Deceased Customers) Directions, 2025 — RBI/2025-26/82** (26 Sept 2025; compliance no later than 31 March 2026).
- **RBI (Commercial Banks – KYC) Directions, 2025 — RBI/DOR/2025-26/169** (28 Nov 2025): accessibility — no mandatory facial gestures, Aadhaar non-mandatory.
- **Fair Lending Practice – Penal Charges in Loan Accounts** (RBI circular 18 Aug 2023; effective 1 Jan 2024): "penal charges" not "penal interest"; now consolidated in RBC Directions 2025.
- **DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 Nov 2025)**: substantive obligations on consent, breach notification (72 hours), retention, erasure **effective ~13 May 2027**.
- **SEBI Master Circular for IAs — SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD-1/P/CIR/2025/003** (8 Jan 2025, in force): explicit call-recording and time-stamping of implementation instructions. Cross-applies to bank IA arms.
- **IRDAI Insurance Fraud Monitoring Framework Guidelines — IRDAI/IID/GDL/MISC/112/10/2025** (9 Oct 2025; effective 1 April 2026): extends to bancassurance.
- **RBI FREE-AI Report** (released 13 Aug 2025): **advisory, not binding** — 7 Sutras and 26 recommendations on responsible AI.

## 4. The 8 conduct themes

1. **Sales & Suitability Conduct** — standards on how products are offered: suitability, consent, anti-mis-selling, script adherence, mis-selling compensation.
2. **Collections & Recovery Conduct** — call recording, time restrictions, agent conduct, distress identification, graduated escalation, data governance for borrower information.
3. **Disclosure & Transparency Conduct** — KFS, APR, penal charges, cooling-off, floating-rate reset, language understood.
4. **Grievance Handling Conduct** — complaint capture, internal review, escalation, RCA, top-5 grounds reporting, external Ombudsman interface.
5. **Vulnerable Customer Protection** — bereaved, MSE, elderly, low-comprehension, persons with disabilities, financially distressed.
6. **Communication & Outbound Conduct** — trilingual coverage, truthful advertising, dark-pattern prohibition, outbound campaign integrity.
7. **Fraud & Unauthorised Transaction Conduct** — fraud handling, customer liability, authentication accountability, alert thresholds, refund timelines.
8. **Third-Party & Outsourcing Conduct** — BPO governance, DSA/DMA conduct, recovery vendor oversight.

## 5. The strongest 12 obligations for Fluid CX

These are the obligations to lead with — chosen by combination of regulatory weight, near-term deadline, and capability strength.

- **OBL-020 — Complaint capture across all customer-interaction channels.** Detect complaints expressed in any channel that never reach CMS. **The anchor obligation — Fluid's "hear the 95% you miss" capability.**
- **OBL-010 — Code of conduct for recovery agents (tone, threats, harassment, public shaming).** Detect threat language, profanity, harassment, customer distress across 100% of recovery calls vs the 2–5% sample-based QA today.
- **OBL-011 — Borrower distress identification mechanism.** Surface distress signals in DPD 1–30 collections calls and whether the agent engaged the hardship pathway. Mandatory from the 12 Feb 2026 draft.
- **OBL-002 — Explicit prior consent for cross-sell; prohibition on compulsory bundling.** Detect bundling language and consent-extraction patterns across 100% of cross-sell interactions.
- **OBL-004 — Outbound telesales script approval and adherence.** Detect script deviation at agent, team, vendor and campaign level across 100% of outbound calls.
- **OBL-007 — Recording of recovery calls with prior borrower intimation.** Detect disclaimer-announcement presence at call start across 100% of recovery calls (recording itself is dialer infrastructure).
- **OBL-012 — "Legal action not as first resort" (graduated escalation).** Reconstruct the borrower's interaction trail across channels and evidence settlement was offered before legal action.
- **OBL-023 — Quarterly Root Cause Analysis to the Customer Service Committee of the Board.** Cluster complaint root causes across the full interaction volume; quarterly board pack ready.
- **OBL-024 — Annual disclosure of top-5 grounds of complaints.** Same engine as OBL-023; year-on-year comparable, defensible to auditors.
- **OBL-027 — Bereaved next-of-kin / deceased customer claims settlement.** Detect empathy markers and tone; surface where bereaved customers got bureaucratic instead of human handling.
- **OBL-037 — BPO / contact-centre vendor governance.** 100% vendor-agent monitoring on the same conduct signals as in-house, producing vendor-level scorecards the supervisor will accept as evidence of governance.
- **OBL-038 — DSA / DMA code of conduct.** Monitor DSA outbound interactions on the same sales-conduct signals; surface DSAs with persistent mis-selling.

## 6. The strongest customer-interaction signals Fluid can detect

These are the detection primitives the dashboard surfaces. Group them and reuse across obligations:

- **Threat and harassment language** — explicit threats ("we'll come to your house"), public-shaming ("we'll tell your office"), repeated escalation, intimidation patterns.
- **Profanity and raised-tone markers** — agent-side civility failures.
- **Customer-distress signals** — "I lost my job", "medical emergency", "my husband died", emotional escalation; pairs with whether the agent engaged or dismissed.
- **Missed-complaint markers** — dissatisfaction language, escalation requests, complaint markers expressed in calls or chats that were never logged in CMS.
- **Bundling and consent-extraction pressure** — "this comes with…", "you must take…", consent buried in account-opening terms, customer-objection overrides.
- **Script deviation** — outbound calls departing from approved campaign scripts; off-script claims about returns, benefits, risks.
- **Mandatory-disclosure absence** — KFS not read out, cooling-off not announced, identification skipped at recovery-call open, time-stamp announcement missing on advisory call.
- **Empathy and tone failures** — bereaved customer handled bureaucratically, distress dismissed, tone deteriorating across the call.
- **Banned-phrase usage** — "penal interest" (instead of "penal charges"), forced-gesture KYC instructions ("please blink twice"), pre-payment-charge quotes where prohibited.
- **Non-borrower contact patterns** — agent confirming caller is not the borrower yet continuing the conversation.
- **Language mismatch** — customer's preferred regional language vs language of service; forced switches.
- **Comprehension-check absence** — agent fails to test whether the customer understood the disclosure.
- **Repeat-contact and graduated-escalation gaps** — sequence reconstruction across channels showing legal action without settlement attempt; status-query loops on document release.

## 9. What Fluid CX supports only with integration ("Monitored with system integration" / "Evidence support only")

These 17 obligations are co-controls. Fluid evidences the conversation side; the named 2LoD or partner system holds the factual decision, the workflow, or the underlying classification.

- **CRM / underwriting / policy partners required:** OBL-001 (suitability — CRM holds the factual classification), OBL-003 (affordability — credit-scoring model holds underwriting), OBL-005 (mis-selling compensation — investigation workflow holds decision), OBL-015 (KFS — document content owned by Product/Legal), OBL-016 (penal charges — Board-approved policy).
- **Dialer / scheduling / vendor / HR partners required:** OBL-007 (recording is dialer infrastructure), OBL-008 (time-restriction enforcement is dialer), OBL-009 (training certificate registry is HR/vendor), OBL-038 (DSA training registry is HR/vendor).
- **Operations / fraud-risk / finance partners required:** OBL-014 (document-release workflow is Operations), OBL-033 (authentication metadata is fraud-risk system), OBL-034 (liability decision and 24×7 line infrastructure).
- **Product / digital / IVR / template partners required:** OBL-029 (video-KYC journey design is Product/Digital), OBL-030 (IVR configuration, template library, multilingual staffing), OBL-031 (Compliance pre-issuance content review).
- **Outbound-correspondence ingestion roadmap dependency:** OBL-018 (most reset notifications are SMS/email), OBL-026 (closure communications mostly written letters/emails).

## 10. What Fluid CX should NOT claim to solve ("Outside Fluid CX scope")

Saying so protects credibility — over-claiming kills RegTech credibility in BFSI.

- **OBL-013 — Data governance for borrower information shared with recovery agents.** Out of scope. This is data-infrastructure access control, vendor data-handling audit, and DPDP compliance — not an interaction-layer problem. Fluid may detect occasional surface signals of unauthorised data reference in agent speech, but the primary control belongs elsewhere.
- **OBL-021 — Internal Ombudsman independent review.** Adjacent. The IO review is human judgment exercised on CMS workflow; Fluid feeds the evidence base but is not the IO function.
- **OBL-022 — Automated CMS with auto-escalation (30 June 2026 deadline).** Adjacent. The bank's CMS provider must deliver the workflow automation. Fluid sits above CMS feeding it discovered complaints — but does not replace CMS workflow.
- **OBL-025 — Principal Nodal Officer + RB-IOS response timelines.** Adjacent. The workflow is correspondence-led; Fluid provides interaction-history context to the PNO but does not run the RB-IOS workflow.
- **OBL-032 — UI audit to prevent dark patterns.** Adjacent. UI/UX audit is a product/design discipline with journey instrumentation. Fluid surfaces complaint signals that reference UI flows, but does not audit the UI itself.
- **OBL-035 — Real-time transaction alerts via SMS/email.** Adjacent. Alert generation is core-banking infrastructure; Fluid sees only the consequence.
- **OBL-036 — Unauthorised transaction refund timelines (credit shadow within 10 days).** Adjacent. Refund workflow and finance system manage the timeline; Fluid surfaces the customer-experience signal around it.
- **OBL-006 — SEBI IA call recording and time-stamping.** Adjacent. The recording infrastructure handles the obligation; Fluid verifies announcement presence but does not perform the time-stamping.

## 11. The strongest dashboard opportunities

These are where the add-on is most differentiated and where it earns the regulatory conversation:

1. **Complaint-capture-vs-CMS reconciliation view.** Surfaces complaints expressed in any channel that never made it into CMS — the data spine for the entire grievance theme and the most defensible artefact for the IO Directions 2026 / RB-IOS 2026 cliff.
2. **Recovery conduct scorecard** (agent × vendor × bucket). Threat/harassment/profanity/distress detection across 100% of recovery calls — the answer to the 12 Feb 2026 draft framework. Includes the borrower-distress engagement flag (OBL-011) which is the operational redefinition of "vulnerability" in lending.
3. **Quarterly board-pack RCA engine** that drives both OBL-023 (CSCB pack) and OBL-024 (annual top-5 grounds). Same engine, year-on-year comparable, methodology-defensible to statutory auditors.
4. **Vendor governance evidence pack** (OBL-037) producing the supervisor-acceptable evidence of governance the Outsourcing Directions 2025 demand — week-on-week vendor-versus-in-house benchmarking.
5. **Pre-legal escalation reconstruction** (OBL-012) — borrower-level journey across calls/SMS/letter/settlement attempts, ready for Legal before SARFAESI initiation.
6. **Suitability and consent execution view** (OBL-001 + OBL-002 + OBL-004) — closes the gap between CRM-recorded suitability and what was actually said on the sales call.
7. **Bereavement-handling empathy view** (OBL-027) — highest-reputational-risk vulnerable-customer journey, with empathy markers, tone trajectory, and repeat-contact patterns.
8. **Fraud-dispute interaction quality monitor** (OBL-033 / OBL-034) — surfaces refused-compensation patterns that may not survive RB-IOS scrutiny under the new ₹30 lakh consequential-loss cap.
9. **Honest-gap shelf.** A dedicated UI region that names the obligations Fluid does NOT solve (OBL-013, OBL-022, OBL-032, etc.) with the partner system that does. This is what a CRO will respect.

## 12. The most important product boundaries (must-not-replace list)

- Fluid is **not the CMS workflow engine** (the 30 June 2026 IO Directions deadline belongs to the bank's CMS provider).
- Fluid is **not the Internal Ombudsman function** (IO is human judgment).
- Fluid is **not the Principal Nodal Officer workflow** for RB-IOS correspondence.
- Fluid is **not the dialer or recording infrastructure** (recording itself, time-stamping, time-window enforcement).
- Fluid is **not the alert engine** (core-banking infrastructure).
- Fluid is **not the refund or credit-shadow workflow**.
- Fluid is **not the UI / dark-pattern auditor** for digital journeys.
- Fluid is **not the data-infrastructure access-control or DPDP compliance layer** for borrower-data handling with recovery agents.
- Fluid is **not the policy-validation layer** for Board-approved penal charges, suitability rules, KFS template content, or campaign creative.
- Fluid does **not record branch interactions today** — the analysis assumes branch calls are recorded as a forward assumption. Three rows in the Control Register (OBL-002 branch leg, OBL-027 branch leg, OBL-029 branch KYC) collapse to outside-scope wherever branch calls are not recorded. Pass 4B explicitly treats branch interaction recording as a precondition the programme creates demand for, not as a separate ask.
