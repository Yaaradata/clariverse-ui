## 0. What changes from HDFC, in one paragraph
**Same as HDFC:** LisN is customer signal intelligence. One meter covers what customers say inside the bank and in public, and what they are actually doing, from bank level down to product. It correlates the two and says what is going wrong. That is the heart of the IndusInd demo too, and the customer pulse leads the home page.

**Different at IndusInd:**
- **The reader and the stakes.** The reader is the CEO's office of a smaller bank in recovery. That office is judged publicly on one target (exit FY27 at about 1% RoA) and one weakness (the cost and mix of deposits).
- **Every pulse row carries its money line,** and the four items that need a decision are sized on IndusInd's own book.
- **The page also shows what is improving.** A page of deficits is the wrong page for this reader.

## 1. Positioning

**One line (screen subtitle and spoken opener):**
> See the bank the way the market and your customers see it, against the peers that matter, and act before it shows in the quarter.

**Tagline:** *Before it shows in the quarter.*

**Category phrase:** customer signal intelligence, at the CEO's altitude. Not "social listening", "sentiment dashboard", "BI" or "market intelligence".

**House spine:** LisN to everything · Distil the signal · Route to the owner. **Value line for IndusInd:** "Act before it reaches the quarter".

**The unit of value, said exactly like this:**
> Something outside moved. A line on our book moved or is exposed. A true peer gives the context. Customers show whether it is reaching them. One owner has a drafted response. A person approves it.

**Why LisN and not what they have:** their MIS shows what happened to balances. Their clippings show what was said. Their analysts can build either on request. LisN joins the outside move to the inside number, sizes it on IndusInd's own book, and hands it to an owner, every week, unprompted. The gap is the join, not a missing feed.

## 4. On-screen copy rules
1. **Every figure carries a date and a layer tag.** The tags are `Public · verified`, `Public · live` and `Internal · illustrative until discovery`.
2. **Every rupee figure opens its arithmetic and base,** and states: "Not a forecast. Not additive across cards."
3. **Peer comparisons name the metric and basis.** Cost of funds never sits beside cost of deposits.
4. **Peer rates appear only beside IndusInd's own rate for the same band.** Never imply that core peers out-pay IndusInd on retail savings.
5. **Owners are roles.** The Internal Ombudsman is "informed", never an owner.
6. **Allegations are labelled "public, unverified", with a date.** Public posts are never counted as complaints.
7. **App ratings carry store, review count and date.** Never compare across stores.
8. **Hindi voice appears in the original, with an English gloss.** No individual is identifiable. Public text is paraphrased; verbatim only if under 15 words and without a handle.
9. **Footer on every screen:** "Every action is a recommendation routed to its owner; LisN does not execute, authorise or decide. Designed to run inside the bank, on the bank's approved models. Public figures from bank and peer disclosures as of [date]; internal figures illustrative until discovery. Data frozen [time]." The watermark runs on every screen as well (IND-B4 §1).
10. **Public management statements may appear attributed to management, with source and date** (the RoA target and its path). Never verbatim, and never framed as a test of the statement. The MD's 150 bp estimate is not used (DEC-3).
11. **Governance themes stay off the default home and Cards screens.** These are SFIO, SEBI, the derivatives matter and former management.

## 6. Objections and answers

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "My team already builds dashboards on our data." | Good: your team owns the internal view. LisN brings what they don't hold. That is every outside source, peer moves and customer voice, joined to your weekly numbers and handed to an owner. It runs on your platform, beside their work. |
| "We have media monitoring and an agency." | They tell you what was said. They don't tell you which balance it touched, what a true peer did that week, or who should act. |
| "Sentiment is noise." | Agreed. We never show a sentiment score. Voice appears only as evidence beside a money line, labelled for reach and source. |
| "Our data can't leave the bank." | It doesn't. It runs in your perimeter, with your keys and read-only access. The first join is weekly aggregates with no personal data. **Weekly deposit data before results is price-sensitive.** For the proof we sign your NDA and accept insider-list designation for our team, as any vendor touching pre-results data does. |
| "How is this different from a CX tool?" | A CX tool manages cases. LisN reads every case and every public post, joins them to what customers then do with their money, and gives the CEO's office the few items that need a decision, each with an owner. |
| "What does RBI expect here?" | The Internal Ombudsman Directions ask for quarterly complaint-pattern analysis. The conduct rules from 1 January make mis-selling a matter of proof. LisN feeds both. We claim no RBI framework beyond those. |
| "What happens when it's wrong?" | Every number traces to its source and arithmetic, and nothing moves without a named person's approval. A wrong item costs one review. Today's mistakes go unseen. |
| "Why these peers? Kotak and RBL are peers." | Federal, Yes and IDFC First are closest on deposit size and mix. Kotak is shown as the upper benchmark. RBL is shown where it matters, on savings and FD rates. The peer set is a setting your team can change. |

## 7. The ask
- **Shape:** a 12-week paid proof on one join. It uses weekly complaint and case counts, plus weekly deposit balances and flows by segment, plus the rate book. These are read against peer rates, adverts, press and customer voice.
- **People:** sponsor is the MD's office or the transformation line. Data owner is the CDO. Users are the CFO, ALCO and the Head, Consumer Banking.
- **Success measures, in business language:**
  - time from an outside event to an owner's decision;
  - every surfaced item traceable to its source;
  - exposure verified by Finance.
  - No profit saving is claimed.
- **Plan:**
  - weeks 1–2: sponsor, owners and scope;
  - weeks 3–5: load and reconcile extracts;
  - weeks 6–10: shadow-run three to five live cards, including Hindi voice;
  - weeks 11–12: executive review and decision.
- **Terms:** NDA and insider-list designation from day one. No price shown.
