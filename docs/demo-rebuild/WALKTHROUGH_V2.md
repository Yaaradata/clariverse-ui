# LisN · HDFC Customer Pulse: walkthrough script (V1 → V2)

For an internal walkthrough with a manager. About 15 minutes.
**Do** = what to click. **Say** = what to say (in your own words).
Before you start, open `http://localhost:3000/role-based/hdfc` and full-screen the browser.

---

## 0. Open (1 min)

**Do:** Stay on the role-based HDFC page.

**Say:**
> "This is the HDFC demo after the second call with Vidya on 25 September. She told us four things. Show the numbers first. Go product by product, the way the bank runs its morning review. Remember a customer across products and channels. And put the bank's most important customers first. So we rebuilt the demo around those four asks.
> We kept the first version as it was, so you can see the difference. Both are listed here as roles under HDFC: V1 is what we showed before, V2 is the new build."

---

## 1. V1 in 30 seconds (1 min)

**Do:** Click **"LisN V1 (first demo)"**. Scroll the page once, top to bottom.

**Say:**
> "This is V1. It opens with a written summary, then themes from public voice: what customers say on X, Reddit, forums and the app stores. It's useful, but it's text-first and bank-wide. It doesn't show the numbers a bank reviews every morning. It doesn't go product by product. And it has no idea who the customer is."

**Do:** Click **"V2 · after Vidya call"** in the header, top right. It switches version and keeps the same screen.

**Say:**

**Say:**

For each one: how many customers have an open issue, how many issues have been open over 5 hours and over 24 hours, and whether the RM knows. On list A, 18 issues are past 24 hours and RMs know about only 6 of 16 affected customers. That's the gap Vidya described."

**Say:**

## 3. One customer, every channel (3 min)

**Do:** Click the **Persona 1** strip, or go to Priority relationships → Persona 1.

**Say:**
> "Persona 1: a fictional private banking customer, with a masked id and no real name. Read the trail from the top.
> Their card is declined at a hotel abroad. An automated bot calls and reads out the reason, but can't raise the limit. Their assistant emails the bank that night. Next morning they call the accounts team about the same payment. At lunch they post publicly to the bank's care handle. In the evening they call again, and a callback is promised but never logged.
> That's six touchpoints across two teams in 23 hours, and the RM was never told."

**Do:** Point at the **purple flag** notes.

**Say:**
> "With LisN, the customer is flagged at touchpoint 2, the unresolved bot call. The flag follows them into accounts, so the next agent sees the history. The email from the assistant is linked through contact details the bank already holds."

**Do:** Scroll to **Recommended actions** and the **CRM task**.

**Say:**
> "Then LisN recommends: alert the RM as a CRM task, call back within 5 hours, and give the customer one owner. LisN recommends; people act. It never executes anything itself."

## 4. Priority relationships (1 min)

**Do:** Click **Priority relationships** in the left menu.

**Say:**
> "The full view: each cohort, negative mentions by channel, products affected, and which RMs were told.
> Two points matter for compliance. First, flags prioritise service; they never restrict or downgrade it. Second, 'high impact' is a property of the complaint, never of the person."

**Do:** Point at **How a customer gets on a list**.

**Say:**
> "This answers 'how do we know who's an RBI official or a celebrity?' We don't work it out. LisN reads the lists the bank already keeps. Separately, a complaint is flagged high-impact if it names the RBI, uses legal language, or comes from a regulator's email domain."

## 6. Deliverables ledger (1.5 min)

**Do:** Click **Are we meeting our deliverables?**

**Say:**
> "We renamed 'promise' to 'deliverables', which is Vidya's word. Each row is a deliverable with its timeline, the compensation due, and how many were met, missed and are still open past the deadline.
> The blue rows are RBI timelines, with the source: failed transactions, card closure, property documents, and complaint resolution before the ombudsman.
> The other rows are timelines the bank sets itself. We show a working assumption, for example 7 working days for card dispatch, marked 'confirm in discovery'. We don't claim to know HDFC's internal SLAs. Once we connect to their systems, these become their real numbers."

---

## 7. Action queue (1 min)

**Do:** Click **Action queue: escalation emails**.

**Say:**
> "This is the 'act' part, from what Pradeep asked about the L2 email team. It holds 20 sample escalation emails in English and Hinglish, each read against its backend status and sorted into six buckets: reply now, call today, escalate, send a proactive status update, close with confirmation, or route to another owner.
> For the 'reply now' bucket there's a draft reply, which a person approves before it's sent. Time saved is an assumption until we have the email team's real numbers."

---

## 8. Where the data comes from (1.5 min)

**Say:**
> "Every tile says which kind of data it shows.
> **Public · live** is real. It covers July to September: Play Store, App Store, Reddit, X and consumer forums, about 28,500 items collected, with 17,193 about HDFC Bank itself. HDFC Life and HDFC Securities are kept out of bank-wide numbers. Names and personal details are removed.
> **Internal · illustrative** is simulated: 5,000 fictional customers and 32,000 interactions across calls, chat, email, branch, WhatsApp and the IVR bot. It follows the same issue mix as public voice, and every total adds up. It shows what they'd see once their data is connected.
> On quality: keyword rules were only about 50% accurate on social posts, so an AI model read and labelled every one of the 11,741 posts. I spot-checked a random sample and agreed with 90% of the labels. Every total reconciles, and a scan checks that no personal data appears on screen."

## Likely questions (keep handy)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is the inside data real? | No. It's simulated and labelled on every tile. It becomes real in discovery. |
| Why only Play Store for replies? | The App Store export has no reply field, and the bank's X replies weren't collected. The screen says so. |
| How do you know who's RBI or a celebrity? | We don't infer it. We read the bank's own lists, and flag the complaint, never the person. |
| Why "Bank TAT: confirm in discovery"? | RBI doesn't set those timelines, and HDFC's internal SLAs aren't public. We show a working assumption until we connect to their systems. |
| How current is the data? | 1 July to 28 September 2026. It rebuilds with one command (`scripts/hdfc_pipeline/run_all.sh`). |
| How accurate is the classification? | Social posts were labelled by an AI model, with 90% agreement on a manual spot check. Store reviews use rules, with sentiment from the star rating. |
| What's not done? | The PayZapp deep dive, the full 1,500-email action queue, and X replies. |
